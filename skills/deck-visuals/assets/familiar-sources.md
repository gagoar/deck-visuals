# Familiar sources — the analogy reference universe

This file is **config, not documentation**. It is the user's curated list of shows,
films, franchises, sports, and internet bits he actually reaches for — the pool the
analogy matcher in `references/levity.md` draws from first. He rotates it by PR:
add a source once he's actually referenced it, remove one that's gone stale, adjust
a `travels-externally` flag once he's tested it on a real external audience.

**The analogy matcher reads this file first.** A claim gets matched here before the
method reaches for anything else. If nothing here fits a claim, say so ("no familiar
source fits") instead of reaching for a franchise that isn't on this list — an
unlisted source might not be one the user knows, and a guessed reference reads worse
than no reference at all.

Sections below cover TV comedy, Animation, Film, Sports, Games, and Internet/memes.
A domain with no seeded source is left present but empty — that is a real gap, not
an oversight. Add to it only from what the user has actually referenced; do not
seed a domain from a guess at his taste.

## TV comedy

**The Office**
- Michael Scott driving into the lake because the GPS said to — trusting a broken
  process past the point it obviously stopped working.
- Kevin's chili spilled across the floor — one small mistake becoming a visible,
  can't-look-away disaster in front of the whole room.
- Michael Scott yelling "I declare bankruptcy" — declaring you've solved a problem by
  saying the magic words, without doing the thing the words require.
- Travels externally: **yes** — long-running syndicated show, broadly recognized
  even outside the US original.

**Friends**
- Ross shouting "PIVOT!" while wedging a couch up a stairwell — brute-forcing a
  change that ignores the constraints and the plan instead of measuring first.
- "We were on a break!" — an argument that never ends because nobody wrote down
  what was actually agreed.
- Monica's one junk-stuffed closet behind an otherwise spotless apartment — a tidy
  surface with the mess pushed one door out of sight.
- Travels externally: **yes** — long-running global syndication, broadly recognized.

**It's Always Sunny in Philadelphia**
- Charlie at the "Pepe Silvia" mailroom wall — string, papers and arrows everywhere,
  frantically explaining a system nobody else can follow. The reference for an
  overloaded diagram / incomprehensible complexity that begs to be simplified.
- Travels externally: **yes** — the conspiracy-board scene is a near-universal
  internet reference, recognized even by people who haven't seen the show.

## TV drama

**Breaking Bad**
- Gus Fring's spotless laundromat sitting on top of a hidden industrial lab — a
  clean, simple front with all the complex machinery deliberately out of view.
- Walt's quiet "Say my name" — the moment the system finally recognizes who owns
  the thing, and says so out loud.
- Walt sure he's in control right up until he isn't — confidence and momentum
  running well past the brakes.
- Travels externally: **yes** — a globally distributed, widely-referenced series.

## Animation

**The Simpsons**
- "The Homer" — the car Homer designs with every conceivable feature bolted on
  (cupholders, a bubble dome, three horns) that ends up unsellable. The reference
  for feature bloat and "more isn't better."
- Homer slowly reversing into the hedge to avoid a conversation — retreating from a
  problem instead of addressing it.
- Travels externally: **yes** — decades of global syndication, close to universal
  recognition.

**Looney Tunes**
- Wile E. Coyote running straight off a cliff and continuing to run on thin air
  until he looks down — momentum and confidence that outran the ground under it.
- The anvil dropping on Coyote right after his plan looked like it worked — a fix
  that immediately backfires.
- Travels externally: **yes** — near-universal recognition across generations and
  regions.

**Futurama**
- Bender's "I'm gonna build my own, with blackjack and…" — walking off to rebuild
  from scratch instead of reusing what's already there.
- Fry slapping cash on the counter, "shut up and take my money" — demand running
  ahead of the thing even being ready.
- Fry's narrow-eyed "not sure if…" squint — polite skepticism, withholding
  judgment until the evidence shows up.
- Travels externally: **yes** — widely syndicated animation with meme-level scene
  recognition.

## Film

**Star Wars**
- The Death Star's unshielded exhaust port — one small weakness nobody reviewed,
  and it takes down the entire system.
- Admiral Ackbar's "It's a trap!" — recognizing a beat too late that you've walked
  straight into a known failure mode.
- "These aren't the droids you're looking for" — something waved through a
  checkpoint without the scrutiny it should have gotten.
- Travels externally: **yes** — near-universal cultural recognition.

**Star Trek**
- Picard's "Make it so" — a decision, once made, handed off to be executed without
  relitigating it.
- The Kobayashi Maru — a test everyone is expected to face, built to see how you
  handle it rather than to be simply "passed."
- Scotty quoting a repair time far longer than he needs, to look like a miracle
  worker when it lands early — padding an estimate for headroom.
- Travels externally: **yes** for the marquee lines; deep-cut episode references
  travel less, so stay with these.

## Sports

**Formula 1**
- A car losing control and crashing at speed — moving fast with no margin for
  error, until there is none left.
- Pit-stop chaos — a normally slick, drilled process falling apart in front of
  everyone the moment one step goes wrong.
- Travels externally: **limited** — a crash reads universally as "this went wrong
  fast," but pit-stop-specific nuance needs some F1 familiarity. Default to the
  crash framing for external decks; save pit-stop chaos for audiences that follow
  the sport.
