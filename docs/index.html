How it works

The pipeline

  • Onboard (out-of-band). A local swipe picker rebuilds the presenter's pop-culture profile. deck-visuals
  • Copy. Draft slide text. Run iceberg on the prose only. iceberg
  • Render. Build or open the deck. frontend-slides
  • Chart injection. dataviz form/marks + brand palette color, inline-SVG + table twin. deck-visuals
  • Concept-visual injection. Diagram, icon, or a deferred placeholder — by the decision rule. deck-visuals
  • Levity injection. GIF fragments at a tasteful cadence. deck-visuals
  • Deliver. Ship to the notes site. deliver-doc

Never a guessed GIF

Levity starts from the slide's claim, not "which GIF." Strip the claim to the shape underneath it — excess, momentum without control, fragility, over-engineering, recurrence, and more — then match one named scene that embodies it, prioritized against what the presenter's own swipe profile says they'd actually recognize.

  • Every GIF is resolved to a direct media URL by search, then run through dv-tools check-media-url — a dead or guessed link never ships.
  • Cadence is disciplined: one at the open, one at a section break, one at the payoff — not one per slide.
  • prefers-reduced-motion swaps in a verified still poster; no GIF is left animating regardless.
  • The picker is the same "Claude proposes, you approve" surface as the palette picker — pick side by side, watch the alternative rack out of focus.

It knows what you'd reference

Matches the shows you love

Swipe through 50 shows and mark what you recognize — recognizing one means you'd get the jokes built around it. What you recognize unlocks accent themes matched to it: house default, a pinned Wes Anderson theme, and 14 more matched to shows like The Matrix, Breaking Bad, and Star Wars. Each theme carries its own coordinated dark surface, hue-matched to the accent — a warm theme never sits on a cold navy floor. It never asks you to name a color — you pick from swatches.
